# Video narrative from a Pokémon teacher to an autonomous player

Editorial summary updated October13UTC,2026. Pete Andrews directs this
AI-assisted project. The honest premise is that the software could finish Red
before the learner could reliably play it without us.

## Opening

“We can make Pokémon Red finish. The harder question is whether a model can
decide what to do, recover when it goes wrong, and finish without us repairing
the run along the way.”

Show a teacher completion next to a retained model decision and failure record.
Do not imply that a scripted victory was an autonomous model breakthrough.

## A concise narrative sequence

1. Build the teacher and reliable controls. Establish cartridge observation,
   controller inputs, state retention and a way to verify real outcomes.
2. Build a battle classroom. Vary teams,moves,levels,HP,PP and conditions in
   disclosed TRAIN fixtures. Show actual learning and retained regressions.
3. Connect the local player. The model selects goals and battle actions; existing
   navigation,PC,nursing and other mechanics execute those choices.
4. Show why “everything is integrated” is not enough. Fresh attempts expose
   source/entry problems, readiness mismatch, bad ranking and recovery gaps.
5. Show useful learning separately from engineering. Ten clean TRAIN examples
   and two earlier fits update the goal model; matched ranks are not unseen
   transfer. Copy qualification does not add wins to the actual save.
6. Show the latest actual progress: Silph,Master Ball,Lapras and Sabrina on one
   retained ancestry, plus Surf/Strength. The party heals and stops at64entries
   and five badges. No new opening replay or copied progress creates this result.
7. End with the unfinished autonomous claim: remaining Red story/registrations,
   then one unchanged fresh completion before any hack or Crystal.
