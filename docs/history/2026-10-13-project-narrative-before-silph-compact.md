## Building the teacher

The first major achievement was software that could finish Red. That solved hard
control problems: reading the cartridge, navigating maps, handling battle menus,
verifying rewards and retaining saves. It also exposed a distinction that still
defines the project: reaching the Hall of Fame does not prove the learner made the
consequential decisions.

An overleveled teacher could win with repetitive attacks and little useful choice.
The collection objective broadened the task to money, storage, encounters,
evolution, gifts, one-off Pokémon and recovery.

## Learning components instead of assuming mastery

The project moved toward short native scenarios with observable outcomes.
Goal models learned from authenticated successes and failures. A battle classroom
made species, levels, stats, moves and full teams available in isolated training,
then tested learned attacks and switching in the actual emulator.

Some candidates improved training error without improving battles. Others reduced
risk while missing an efficiency screen. Those results remain in the record;
successful later use does not retroactively turn an earlier failed test into a pass.

## Why progress has felt repetitive

Successful components have not always become a durable working configuration.
