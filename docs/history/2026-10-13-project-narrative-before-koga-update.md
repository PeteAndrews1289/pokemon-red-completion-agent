# Project story from a teacher to a Pokémon player

Pete Andrews directs this AI-assisted project to build a locally running,
learned Pokémon player. The recurring question is not just whether Red finishes:
it is who makes the decisions, what was learned, and how much help was needed.

## The product, in order

Finish one fresh model-directed Red campaign with Champion/Hall-of-Fame proof
and all124 legitimate registrations on its declared same-save route. No runtime
LLM, final-player cheating, stitched saves or copied victories count. Deterministic
navigation and disclosed mechanics may support consequential learned choices.

Then test an unfamiliar compatible ROM modification, learn Crystal and continue
the registered Pokédex through at least Emerald. Version, trade and event
dependencies are recorded rather than invented. This is a registration goal,
not a requirement to retain every evolutionary form simultaneously.

## What the latest work teaches us

An exact route has to end at the next executor's real entry tile. Reaching the
right building elsewhere is not sufficient. The complete Silph handoff now
quotes and verifies that boundary on copies and actual play.

A menu can bias sampling simply by offering many variants of one task. Public
option-kind averaging corrects that bias without forcing story order or changing
the network's scores. Optional preparation and paid collection still interleave.

Normal outcomes also need honest handling. Escort XP is not trainee progress,
but a safe zero-trainee-XP return should not kill the session. Poison can reduce
HP while walking; such a trip must not be quoted as an unchanged-HP executor.
The repairs preserve exact resource checks and use ordinary nursing.
