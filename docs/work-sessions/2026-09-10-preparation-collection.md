# Faster preparation, three lessons and no new registration

## Mission check

- Capability: sustain missing-registration collection with less repeated preparation.
- Learned authority: actual sampled goals/destinations; the preparation repair adds no authority.
- Transfer test: fresh states in the same training lineage, not independent or cross-title evidence.
- Cheapest falsifier: changed history passes verification, or searches cannot produce useful collection.
- Time box: at most 30 minutes of preparation maintenance, then four goals with a 1,200-second limit checked between goals and unchanged per-goal safety bounds.
- Stop: authentication/safety failure, first non-search failure or declared limits. No resets or consumed retries.

## Preparation repair and measurement

Continuation preparation opened every ancestor once to read its header, then opened it again
for the checkpoint join. The existing private episode reader already retains an authenticated
in-memory snapshot of all streams. The repair reuses that snapshot within the same preparation.

The checkpoint still verifies its sealed record, captured state, header, terminal and trajectory
manifest. The next preparation opens and validates the files again. There is no persistent
path/mtime cache, and no snapshot is carried across controller input by this integration.

That is a **36.9% reduction in this single preparation comparison**, not a repeated benchmark
or a claim that whole-batch training is 36.9% faster. Startup and prior training-inventory
validation remain substantial costs. The first after-check stopped at the published-source
requirement before preparation; the measured after-check ran only after the tested source was pushed.

Targeted tests verify one open per ancestor, fresh validation on the next call, rejected edits
to header/checkpoint/events/manifest, mismatched parent/profile/record/episode rejection, and
