# The fitted Red learner makes its first played decision

## What happened

The separately declared first-choice training probe completed Bruno. The new
four-example forward model chose **advance story** from two genuine alternatives,
rather than spend another healing item. Its selected cartridge skill executed
181 controller actions and 17,293 emulator frames, reached the current verified
Bruno objective, and retained all 30 specimens: 28 living species, 33 registered.
No field item was spent, no second choice was needed, and no exception occurred.

This is model-directed high-level control using deterministic movement and battle
skills—not learned button-level play or a completed full-game agent.

The starting point was a successful historical one-heal checkpoint, explicitly
restored for a new correlated training probe. Its current resources differed from
the first-choice context used to fit the model, but its lineage and continuation
history were already observed. Neither an independent validation root nor a new
untouched test is claimed. The previous practice and four-lesson pilots stay closed.

## Did the new model actually choose?

Yes. The immutable role log records one `forward_first` choice, the expected
forward-model identity, one-hot selection, and the matching executed story option.
The old model's read-only diagnostic preferred another heal. That recommendation
was not executed and was not used to override the learner. The new head estimated
completion/cost at approximately 0.9836/0.01293 for story versus 0.6773/0.21714 for
healing. These regression outputs are **not calibrated success probabilities**.

Only frozen model82 could have chosen a second action, using its independently
declared seed. The goal ended the attempt after the first action, so the tail was
not queried. The completed trace and checkpoint agree on the new first-actor hash;
the old tail hash remains separately recorded. The private post-run audit also
verified terminal identity and checkpoint joins without an emulator or input.

## What this does not establish

- No comparison against an actually played old-policy control on this state.
- No measured efficiency advantage, reliable win rate, or calibrated confidence.
- No new native-fitting row, fit, production promotion, or recursive deployment.
- No evidence that this Bruno-specific return predicts Agatha or Champion returns.
- No concurrent Champion/Hall-of-Fame evidence; **Phase 4 is not complete**.

The earlier current Champion failure remains a separate retained history. No
sealed Red, Crystal or full-game replay occurred. The long-term target remains
story completion and living Pokédex development, then adaptation to other games.

## Engineering review

The source was published as `669cefa319dcabf77ceab2dcd08f65bde1738530` after
655 focused runtime/learner tests, 109 product/roadmap tests, repository lint and
static typing of 450 source files. Full regression and hosted CI were launched
against that exact frozen source; those results remain separate until complete.

The internal reviewer tested role separation and found a checkpoint/header hash
mismatch before any live input. Codex corrected it and added regression coverage.
Unsupported or partial menus, initial singletons, ambiguous observer flags,
uncertain durable writes and consumed claims fail closed without retries.

The important next question is not whether this saved state can be beaten again.
It is how to collect genuinely different, prospective situations while keeping
the goal and continuation contract honest. Do not silently relabel another boss,
use training error as confidence, or resume a broad teacher-repair cascade.
