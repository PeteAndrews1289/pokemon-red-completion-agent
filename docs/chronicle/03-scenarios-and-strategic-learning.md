# 3. August 15–September 5: finding useful questions and trustworthy outcomes

[Previous](02-evaluation-and-refocus.md) · [Contents](README.md) · [Next](04-continuous-collection.md)

## A collection of saves is not automatically a curriculum

Short scenarios removed the need to replay a whole game for every question.
They introduced another problem: which states actually ask a useful question,
and which can legitimately be used for training or evaluation?

The August archive repeatedly distinguishes independent origins from related
saves, real alternatives from single permitted actions, and a failed setup from
an observed model outcome. A state with fourteen possible labels is not fourteen
independent experiences. A zero-input preflight can protect an experiment without
producing any learning.

This period accumulated extensive provenance, one-shot declarations, source
authentication and failure-retention machinery. Some attempts stopped before
inputs or fitting. Those are engineering outcomes, not hidden successes.
The records retain them rather than awarding progress because a gate ran.

That rigor also had a cost. Many sessions produced authenticated preparation
rather than new model behavior. The history should show this tension, not turn
every completed protocol into a breakthrough.

## Portable concepts and authentic Red execution

The August 20–21 work explored dependency reasoning in constructed, rootless
situations before connecting those concepts to Red.
The [campaign](../evidence/rootless-living-dex-dependency-campaign-result-v1-2026-08-20.json),
[fit](../evidence/rootless-living-dex-dependency-fit-result-v1-2026-08-20.json) and
[later comparison](../evidence/rootless-living-dex-dependency-v2-comparison-result-2026-08-21.json)
are separate steps. Abstract success did not automatically grant native control.

Authentic Red choices followed, along with failures at the seam between the
semantic question and executable skill. Some proposed options could not be
materialized at the required boundary. Some failures lacked enough retained
detail to justify a repair. The active-state history keeps these closed lanes
rather than silently recycling their identities.

The eventual direction was observed-outcome learning: execute a real selected
goal, retain its actual costs and result, and learn from that evidence. Do not
label an unexecuted alternative as if its outcome were known.

## September 2–3: the battle result that said to stop

A fixed battle roster failed an information gate before gameplay or fitting.
The response changed scenario supply rather than weakening the claimed test.

The subsequent archive describes native battle scenarios with controlled
variation and repeated RNG conditions. A challenger selected 18 of 20 preferred
actions against the prior's 17, while a simple legal heuristic selected all 20.
Those were opening-action experiments with declared continuations, not proof
of whole-battle mastery.

The project did not need another victory declaration for a slightly improved
model that still lost to the baseline. The decision shifted attention upward:
choose whether to acquire, evolve, develop the team, recover or pursue a
prerequisite. Battle mechanics could support that hierarchy.

Later battle work revisited the problem under a different whole-battle question.
That is a change in experimental scope, not grounds to erase this negative result.

## A strategic choice made a measurable difference

A small learned manager was connected to executable semantic goals. Read-only
checks first established that it could see real alternatives. Agreement with
a fixed manager was useful integration evidence, but not an advantage.

A paired development comparison then exposed an actionable difference. The
learned manager chose team development and finished in 1,119 actions. The fixed
completion-first alternative pursued an evolution that did not finish within
6,000 actions. An earlier pair had revealed that budget exhaustion was being
treated as a crash; the repaired comparison retained it as a bounded outcome.

The [third-pair result](../evidence/red-causal-player-pair-003-result-2026-09-03.json)
is therefore a real but narrow strategic milestone: one starting situation,
different choices, shared mechanics, measured completion. It is not proof of
robust long-horizon play or transfer.

## Reusing evidence without reusing the exam

By September 4–5, inventories separated reusable training contexts from unopened
evaluation material. Related resets could be useful lessons if honestly grouped;
they were not independent worlds. This replaced an overly expensive demand for
a wholly new origin for every classroom example.

The lesson was balance: preserve source identity and untouched evaluation,
but do not make provenance so cumbersome that the learner receives no experience.
