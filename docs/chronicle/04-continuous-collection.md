# 4. September 6–10: continue from failure, evolve, and redefine collection

[Previous](03-scenarios-and-strategic-learning.md) · [Contents](README.md) · [Next](05-economy-and-runtime.md)

## The next lesson starts where the last attempt ended

The September 6 work shifted toward continuing an earned state rather than
restoring an earlier convenient situation after every failure.
The [saved-endpoint learning report](../work-sessions/2026-09-06-saved-endpoint-learning.md)
records one negative search outcome admitted into fitting: 31 examples became 32.
The following diagnostic search caught nothing and stopped safely.

That is not collection success or evidence of an improved policy. It is a useful
infrastructure property: unsuccessful play can produce an honest result, preserve
its spending, and become experience without pretending the species was caught.

The archive's many changing model numbers describe versions and counts under
their own contracts. They are not a single transferable measure of project
completion and should not be added across different learners.

## Evolution exposed dependencies that a catch counter hides

Collection requires more than encountering a missing species. A precursor may be
in storage. It may have no useful attack. A needed teammate may carry an HM.
Training may reach the required level without returning to a boundary where the
next skill can operate. Healing and relocation can cost more than the battle.

The September 7 reports on
[resumable evolution](../work-sessions/2026-09-07-resumable-evolution.md),
[party selection](../work-sessions/2026-09-07-party-selection.md) and
[complete evolution learning](../work-sessions/2026-09-07-complete-evolution-learning.md)
show why apparently small collection goals repeatedly crossed several systems.

Rapidash and other acquired or evolved species were visible progress. Equally
important was the distinction between a useful duplicate and a new registration.
A second precursor could support an evolution dependency without deserving
another first-time-species reward.

Storage, capture, healing and evolution had to operate on the same actual party
and inventory. A goal could not succeed merely because a local component returned
a favorable message.

## Scope changed on September 9

Pete retired simultaneous living specimens and the level-100 stretch target.
The enduring goal became a shared verified registration ledger across games:
earn a species legitimately once globally, while retaining separate local Pokédex
flags and actual physical specimens.

This was a product decision, not a retrospective regrading of old results.
The [shared-registration plan](../shared-pokedex-registration-plan.md) and
[mission](../../MISSION.md) preserve the migration. Earlier living-Dex receipts
remain historical evidence under their original rules.

The revised objective removed pointless repetition but not necessary dependencies.
A globally known species might still be useful for the current team or to reach
a missing evolution branch. Unavailable version, event or trade dependencies
would later be made explicit rather than fabricated into Red's save.

## September 10: the record grew faster than the reader could follow

As development accumulated, public documents became stacks of competing “Current”
sections. The README had grown to 4,165 lines and the operational handoff to
12,928. A reader could find enormous detail while still not know what was true now.

The [documentation reset](../work-sessions/2026-09-10-public-project-reset.md)
moved accumulated material into history, checked archive bodies against their
sources, and replaced entry points with concise summaries. The original tree
remains available at commit `9e7c2e9c`.

That cleanup preserved evidence, but later exposed a different failure: readers
could no longer easily see the whole story in the current narrative. The chronicle
you are reading addresses that discoverability problem without restoring giant
operational logs.

## What this phase established

The project was building a continuing player, not merely a library of isolated
skills. It had actual negative outcomes, preserved endpoints, dependency-aware
collection and an explicit change in what completion meant.

The remaining problem was sustainability. A player that can travel, catch and
evolve still has to afford the next attempt.
