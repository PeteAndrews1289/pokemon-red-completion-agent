## Why grinding was not the complete answer

A read-only review found seven choices of a reserve with zero positive-power
coverage while another available option had coverage, nine type-immune attacks,
and four switches below quarterHP. At one optional switch prompt, a returning
Gengar had2HP. The model chose Normal-only Snorlax instead of keeping Blastoise;
its next attack, Headbutt, did no damage through the type immunity.

These are concrete decision risks, not proof that a particular alternate battle
would have won. Coverage calculations omit indirect damage and future tactics;
sleep, enemy switching, critical hits and other randomness still matter. Nor
can the earlier four-knockout loss and later three-knockout loss establish that
grinding made play worse: party composition, resources and trajectories differ.

The important distinction is that useful information was already in the features.
The proposed next step is measured TRAIN-only switching/control practice with
damage and status components held fixed, including cases where staying is best.
No developer-selected battle action, immunity mask or save edit was inserted to
make the real game pass. No DEVELOPMENT episode became a fitting label.
