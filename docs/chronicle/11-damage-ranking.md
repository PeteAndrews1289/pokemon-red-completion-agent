# September 23: learning which attack actually works

The Route22 rival loss exposed a useful question. The party could reach the
battle, fight and recover, but the damage shortlist sometimes excluded the move
that would hurt the opponent most. Better navigation or more money would not
explain that choice.

The first outcome-trained replacement looked encouraging in aggregate, then
failed its own checks: particular levels, stat ratios and one matchup family
became worse. A crossed diagnostic found that this rejected head chose special
attacks in every one of208old and new configurations. That was evidence for a
curriculum and representation problem, not permission to ignore the failed groups.

The next design used eight interactions between observable power, effectiveness,
stats, accuracy and current resources. A small linear head learned its coefficients
from native damage outcomes. The curriculum balanced physical-favored and
special-favored families before seeing their outcomes. The teacher could construct
the starting states, while the fitted model could only rank ordinary legal moves.

The first implementation attempt stopped because the written packet requested
timing offsets beyond the runner's supported limit. Sixteen forced measurements
and the failure remain retained. A separate V2 fixed that contract, excluded all
of the first plan's species pairs and completed one fit without retries.

On48fresh comparison configurations, average damage regret fell from0.13965
to0.00906. The candidate chose an exact-best move in41cases against the old
head's27, and every reported comparison group improved. No family regressed.
The96older damage configurations also improved. The reviewer reconstructed all
4320forced branches,37,920event records and the fitted coefficients.

This is the first passing result for this particular balanced interaction
successor. It does not mean the model won41of48battles: these were one-turn
measurements of all four alternatives. The cases reused four TRAIN origins,
and nine timing offsets were correlated samples. Complete battles still have
status effects, switching, diminishing HP/PP and moves such as Dig and Fly that
were excluded here.

The result earned a frozen candidate for that next full-battle comparison.
The story and collection saves did not move, and the deployed battler did not
change. The practical advance was narrower and useful: the damage learner
passed the check that its predecessor had failed.

## Later that day: did better damage ranking survive a whole battle?

The frozen head was placed beside the existing status, control and switching
heads, with no new fit. Both complete actors played24fresh constructed teams at
three timing offsets. Every attack and replacement belonged to the model.

The candidate won38of72battles; the admitted actor won18. In six-versus-six
teams the comparison was19wins against7. The candidate still lost34times—this
was a stress bank, not an ordinary story win-rate estimate or a perfect player.
On the17battles both won, the candidate used19.4%fewer turns and20.3%less PP.
It fainted less often and defeated more opponents. All frozen gates passed.

The audit replayed2825choices from their saved observations and checked11190
event records. Status moves, voluntary switches, forced replacement and all six
party slots appeared. One audit assumption needed correction: victory clears
the foe roster, so the final knockout must join battle-exit and HP evidence.
No battle was rerun to repair that reporting issue.

This is the important bridge from a good damage score to a useful complete
battler. It still uses four existing TRAIN origins and correlated timing variants.
Dig/Fly compatibility remains a separate question before narrow story admission.
The deployed actor, story and collection saves remained unchanged.

## Two-turn moves passed—and the audit corrected our PP measure

The same frozen candidate then completed a separate Dig/Fly packet:16prescribed
execution probes and48full learned battles. Both actors won6of24. The candidate
freely chose Dig11times and Fly40times;31charge releases and3interruptions were
observed, without an extra policy choice during player charging. All264learned
choices and5285events were checked. Common-win frames fell16.5% and actual PP
spending20%. Overall candidate frames were slightly higher; compatibility is not
optimal charge valuation or a guarantee of winning the next story battle.

The audit found that some old TRAIN origins heal after victory, restoring PP
before the final endpoint. Our historical PP metric was positive endpoint
depletion, not actual spending. Recounting authenticated executor proofs changes
the previous composition's common-win PP improvement from20.3%to18.6%.
The38versus18wins and19.4%turn reduction are unchanged, and both packets still
pass. We preserved the original records and added this correction; there was no
replay. Terminal HP may also contain native healing, not just battle survival.

All three planned checks now pass: damage ranking, complete battles and bounded
two-turn execution. The next step is a narrow, explicitly bound story trial—not
another damage fit. Saves and the deployed actor stayed unchanged during testing.

## Back to the earned game: the rival finally fell

The next session connected that exact frozen model to the story interface.
Identity checking, legal status moves and logged authority moved together;
ordinary funding and League behavior stayed unchanged. The operator declared
the required Route22 integration target, while every combat choice belonged to
the model. Travel and recovery remained disclosed deterministic support.

The candidate defeated all six opponents in26decisions:12attacks,7voluntary
switches,2forced replacements and5switch prompts. Two party members fainted.
It earned3445cash, then recovered the whole team at Viridian's Pokemon Center.
The26choices and100events were independently checked against the frozen actor;
the durable victory flag, actual reward and healed save were verified without
new inputs. It was a real win at the immediate story blocker.

The previous loss and19694cash cost were not erased. These were not paired
trials, so the contrast is not a causal win-rate estimate. Story remains29/36:
beating the rival is a prerequisite, not the whole Victory Road objective.
No weights were fitted and no registrations were added. Next come the earned
route's puzzles and trainer boundaries, with explicit model combat ownership.
