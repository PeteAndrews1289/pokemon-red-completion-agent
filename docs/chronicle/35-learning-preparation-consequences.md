# September28: learn the consequence, not just the training action

The project could already offer training for a particular party member, earn XP,
heal, and challenge Misty. What remained missing was a learning target that joined
the preparation's cost to the subsequent challenge's actual result.

A six-arm experiment compared fighting immediately, healing first, or training a
named member. Setup required a narrow extension:the battle classroom's synthetic
party could not safely double as a field-training fixture because its member
fingerprints collided. Pete approved distinct,stable TRAIN-only party identities;
the existing party reader and battle guards were preserved.

The injured party lost when sent directly to Misty,but won after healing. The
already-prepared healthy party won without more training. Both three-level
grinding assignments earned genuine XP yet stopped short of their targets under
the declared limits. Those shortfalls were retained as unreached challenges,
not recorded as battle losses or silently rerun until success.

All six native outcomes entered one separate consequence-model fit. Its recorded
injured-party ranking changed from immediate battle to healing. This was actual
outcome-based training,but not a deployed autonomous decision:the experiment's
six interventions were teacher-assigned,and its states shared one TRAIN ancestry.
The old models and real Cerulean save were unchanged.

The remaining gap became more specific. There was still no successful earned
training-dose-then-Misty example,so the new head could not establish when grinding
helps. The next proposal narrowed the dose to one level and retained the same
actors,limits and failure accounting. No new battle architecture was needed.

game input;the failure remained visible,and only the six unused assignments ran
