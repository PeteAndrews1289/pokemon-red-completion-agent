# 7. September 21: connect gifts, continued decisions, and earned story play

[Previous](06-battle-classroom.md) · [Contents](README.md) · [Next](08-preparation-and-routing.md)

## Gifts needed to belong to the player's real menu

A skill that can receive Lapras is not yet a player that knows when to pursue it.
The gift work connected outcomes to the ordinary goal model's actual context and
reward calculation, rather than treating a convenient teacher action as a sampled
model choice.

An earlier candidate failed old-row retention. The accepted Model141 update
kept 137 prior outcomes and added four explicitly identified TRAIN teacher lessons.
Those four rows are not four new autonomous comparative decisions.

The [gift qualification](../evidence/red-gift-model-qualification-2026-09-21.json)
records a real selection among five alternatives: Lapras was chosen, travel and
receipt completed, and registration increased while party and cash were preserved.
That closed a bounded integration question, not all gift mechanics or planning.

## The player continued when searches did not pay off

The [integrated episode](../evidence/red-integrated-player-2026-09-21.json)
tested continued decisions after ordinary unsuccessful goals. Seven decisions
included five failed searches and a successful boxed Abra exchange for Mr. Mime.
The final search reached its encounter limit. All 3,000 spent remained counted.

This is stronger than showing one good choice from a carefully posed menu:
the player had to choose again after the world and resources changed. It is still
one short episode, not efficient collection or a reliable full-game policy.

The separate collection lineage reached 109/124 native registrations and 78
specimens with 11,968 cash. Model141's retained evidence totals were 141 fitted
outcomes, 96 successes and 58 economy-qualified outcomes. These figures measure
specific ledgers; none is an overall-project completion percentage.

## An ownership audit changed the story claim

The audit separated a trained collection manager from an early-story conductor
whose evidence was integration-only. Existing story routes did not automatically
inherit the collection model's learned strategic authority.

A plural story binding then exposed Hideout and Saffron as genuine alternatives.
The [story-branch probe](../evidence/red-story-branch-development-2026-09-21.json)
records Model141 choosing Hideout, obtaining the Silph Scope and returning safely.
Five battles and healing remained scripted support.

The probabilities were nearly even. The result proved that a model choice
controlled the chapter that was played; it did not establish strategic superiority.
No healing decision occurred, so learned healing was not declared solved.

## Earned story play met stronger opposition

The story lineage continued separately from the collection save. Trainer funding,
transport and recovery had to work with its actual money and team rather than
borrow the collection lineage's stronger position.

Silph rival failures exposed weaknesses that earlier easy trainer encounters had
not established. Losses and cash costs remained recorded. Preparation, battle
integration and status behavior became the immediate bottlenecks.

The ensuing work did not mean the entire battler had been useless. It meant its
qualified workload was narrower than the next story challenge. A model that wins
early natural battles has not automatically demonstrated reliable multi-opponent
attrition or status judgment.

## Keep the ledgers separate

At the end of this phase, two different achievements mattered: a collector that
could continue after failed goals, and a story player beginning to connect learned
combat to earned progression. They were not one stitched fresh run.
