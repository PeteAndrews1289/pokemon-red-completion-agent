This is the enduring development history of Pete Andrews's AI-assisted Pokémon
player project. It follows the completion-agent repository from July 28 through
October14,2026. The initial history was reconstructed on September22 from retained prose,
dated reports, experiment receipts and Git history—not from an assumed perfect
memory of the conversations.

The story is about a changing answer to one question: **who is actually playing?**
A teacher can finish Red while a learner does very little. A battle model can win
while navigation cannot reach the next opponent. A collector can add a species
while its money supply becomes unsustainable. These distinctions shaped the work.

## Evidence rules for reading this history

“Learned” does not mean every button, destination or preparation decision was learned.
Each chapter identifies the authority actually exercised. TRAIN, DEVELOPMENT and
final acceptance are separate; sibling states and timing variants are not independent
origins. A failed comparison stays failed even if the owner later permits narrow use.

Dates refer to the cited record or explicit archive heading. Archive order is not always
chronological; some headings describe an earlier experiment or later correction.
Where the sources conflict, retain the conflict and explain the correction. Do not
silently combine changing rosters, metrics or collection definitions into one curve.

The July successor repository is the starting boundary here. The earlier
`pokemon-red-ai` project is acknowledged as a predecessor, but its experiments and
results are not imported as this project's achievements. Conversation-only details,
exact total coding hours, service-wide model usage and missing recordings have not
been reconstructed. No novelty or statistical-significance claim is added by this work.

## Completion-first development log

storytelling and, if useful, a more rigorous publication rerun. Product development
comes first. The existing dated reports in `docs/work-sessions/` are the running
log; this chronicle and the video narrative summarize them at useful milestones.
The historical `docs/worklog.md` remains an archive, not a second current log.

For each meaningful work session, retain this compact record in its existing report:

- **Purpose and decision:** the player outcome sought and why this approach was chosen.
- **Changes and reuse:** affected components, model/configuration and source-state
  identities; identify dirty working changes when a commit alone is insufficient.
- **Attempts and outcomes:** successes, failures and recovery, measured costs/time
  when available, and links to retained raw logs or receipts. Unknown is not zero.
- **Authority and shortcuts:** model choices versus programmed or operator assistance,
  training-state edits, relaxed checks and reused tests. Label exploratory results.
- **Repeat efficiently:** the shortest demonstrated procedure, prerequisites,
  components to reuse, and dead ends to avoid. Separate tested steps from hypotheses.
- **Validate later:** unresolved limitations and the fresh comparisons or measurements
  a stronger claim would need; do not claim they have already been performed.

Capture raw runtime evidence as work happens; batch the explanatory prose rather
than interrupting every small repair. Link reusable commands/configurations instead
of duplicating them. Keep ROMs, saves, model files and private machine paths out of
public documents; use artifact identifiers and sanitized evidence references.
Preserve existing histories and failed attempts. No new logging framework, mandatory
video recording, paper-writing phase or extra experiment is required by this request.

A later publication run would use a separately stated protocol and fresh evaluation
evidence. This log can shorten setup and explain the discoveries; it cannot recover
missing measurements or turn repeated development tests into independent validation.
