# September27: learning a distinction is not yet choosing well

At Cerulean,the first question was whether the model could even distinguish two
story choices. With generic costs matched,the old representation treated their
different challenges alike. Adding portable opposition features solved that
representation problem,but their initial zero weights changed no predictions.

The native lessons were less flattering than the plumbing. Six disclosed TRAIN
fixtures sent Wartortle against Misty at different levels and health. All six
lost. Replaying the recorded model rankings and move-slot effects found no
executor substitution:the battler really had selected those moves. Those losses
remained lessons rather than being replaced by hand-picked successes.

Bill supplied another useful distinction. Asking for help,operating the machine
and receiving the ticket were three real interactions,but only the ticket
completed the quest. Incremental proof allowed the first two to teach useful
progress without inventing full completion. These fixtures skipped prerequisites
explicitly;they were not an earned trip through Nugget Bridge.

A separate mechanics copy then healed and won eight Route4wild battles. It
earned639XP and returned safely,but stayed at level18 below its declared20target.
That was partial preparation,not a completed training goal or learned grinding
decision. The original party roles and completed earlier target remained intact.

Finally,one bounded retention selection incorporated all141older examples and
23authenticated additions. The new164-example candidate reduced error on the
additions by92.4%,while older-example error rose1.9%,within the declared2%limit.
On a known matched-cost menu,it now scored the two challenges differently and
preserved the scores when their order reversed.

This was genuine parameter learning,not proof of better play. No independent
comparison had run,no candidate had been adopted,and the real Cerulean endpoint
still had both quests unfinished. The next test is whether those learned
distinctions improve native decisions—not whether we can fit the same lessons
again or force a favorable route.
