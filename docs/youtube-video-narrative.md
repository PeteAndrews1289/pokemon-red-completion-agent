## The story arc

### 1. Decide what “playing Pokémon” means

Explain the target before showing the engineering: a fresh model-directed Red
run, Champion and Hall of Fame verified, and all 124 registrations on the declared
legitimate route earned in that same save. Later reliability needs at least eight
successes in ten preregistered held-out clean starts. Neither has been proved.
[Mission](../MISSION.md) · [Completion contract](completion-contract.md).

Show the decision pipeline: semantic observations → local goal and battle models
→ declared execution skills → native outcomes. This is not pixel-only play.
Coding assistants help develop the system; the intended player makes no runtime
LLM calls. Movement, menus and other supplied mechanics need explicit credit.
[Architecture](architecture.md) · [Assistance policy](assistance-policy.md).

### 2. Build a teacher, then separate the student's credit

Show why a reliable teacher was useful: it supplied controls, game knowledge and
reproducible situations in which a student could learn. Then label the boundaries.

action. Diagnostic XP, money or Pokémon never enter the actual campaign.
[Development contract](../NORTH_STAR.md).

Show their real achievements alongside their repairs, restoration and assistance.
Keep each ancestry separate. In-game levels are gameplay; a fitted parameter
change is model learning. A successful supported save does not establish an

### 4. Ask whether training is actually useful

Pete's question gives this chapter its focus: why spend so much time fighting weak
wild Pokémon? Follow the full cost of preparation, including travel, PP, healing,
losses and the decision to stop. A win can still be unproductive for the trainee.
[Preparation and time-loss session](work-sessions/2026-10-26-masked-native-validation.md).

On one exposed related source and seed, the combined candidate completes the same
evolution/Misty sequence with 7,460 → 1,282 actions, 538,368 → 94,428 frames,
21 → 3 restores and 12 → 2 faints. The trainee's full-sequence XP is
1,960 → 1,993; incidental total party XP falls. This is a local completed-sequence
comparison, not a whole-game speed result or a gain attributable solely to the
32-weight update.
[Measured comparison and attribution](evidence/red-masked-native-validation-2026-10-26.json).

## October27, later: a correct fit can still choose the wrong battle

The preparation consequence learner first exposes a retention/calibration tradeoff.
Two corrected candidates fail their unchanged quality gates and remain rejected.
A narrow opt-in utility budget and a joint feasibility check then produce a third
head that keeps pooled old15 utility-MAE and passes all17-row TRAIN gates.
The third model reduces local utility MAE0.404192→0.359571. The data still comes
from one related origin; this does not prove broad learning.

The decisive native test is negative. The qualified head is demonstrably used,
yet challenges Misty twice instead of preparing and loses both times. Eight model
choices finish without execution errors; no learned preparation or stopping is
verified. Keep this distinction visible: running the model, fitting its lessons
and playing well are separate accomplishments. The next useful question is why
the measured TRAIN preference fails to carry into the actual offered choices.
No actual campaign progress or global model adoption is added.
