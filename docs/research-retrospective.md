# Research retrospective and prospective paper record

Snapshot: September 22, 2026, earned story stage 285. This is an evidence map and a
prospective research plan, not a paper, a novelty claim or a completed benchmark.
The initial documentation refresh ran no gameplay; the subsequent routing session

## Research question and system boundary

Can small learned goal and battle policies, supported by explicit game-control skills,
become a sustained Pokémon player and carry useful knowledge between titles?
Red is the development environment. No unfamiliar-hack or Crystal gameplay transfer
has been established. Candidate contributions are questions to investigate, not claims
of originality: continuation-aware training, auditable authority transfer, and the
composition of learned decisions with resource-sensitive long-horizon execution.

Pete Andrews directs the product, criteria and development choices. AI coding assistants
help implement and review it. At runtime, small NumPy learners select supported semantic
goals or battle actions; they are not LLMs choosing every button. Observation uses
privileged emulator/cartridge facts, not pixels alone. Navigation, menus, masks, safety
checks and several strategic preparation choices remain programmed support.
[Architecture](architecture.md) defines the current allocation of responsibility.

   J and K experiments separated continuation assumptions rather than hiding the difference.

   Battle completion and strict funding acceptance had to be separate contracts.

   profitability remained unproven. Selling items is not the declared default funding strategy.

   More executable skills alone were insufficient evidence of strategic model authority.

   failure. Learned Silph/gym results followed; final-player acceptance did not change.

## Reproducibility and artifact inventory

Existing public JSON receipts expose outcomes, source/terminal hashes and protocol details.
Private ROMs, saves, datasets and model artifacts are not distributed. Hashes authenticate
available artifacts; they do not make absent artifacts independently reproducible.
The preparation checkpoint used local commit `3e20ef8f`; later routing source checkpoints
are `4dd2b21d` and `24528a87`. Receipts retain actual execution identities. The public
publication snapshot does not replace those identities or make every local commit publicly
reachable. A documentation commit is not another experiment.

For future material experiments, retain or explicitly mark missing:

- Source commit, interpreter/dependency versions, adapter/cartridge identity and lawful ROM hash.
- Exact model/config/receipt hashes, ancestry, partition and frozen eligibility rules.
- Origin, RNG/timing conditions and seed where controlled; distinguish unknown randomness.
- Prospective budgets, stopping rules, comparisons, metrics and acceptance/rejection decisions.
- Action masks, observations, selected actions, probabilities where available, decision latency,
  terminal checks, resource changes, faults and all interventions.
- Complete lineage and failure/attrition counts, not only successful endpoints.
- Game execution time separately from analysis, human and coding-agent time and metered cost.
- Artifact availability and lawful reuse conditions; no ROM or private-path publication.

Do not infer AI-agent usage savings from elapsed time or number of delegated tasks.
Earlier Flash experiments may inform workflow design; their quantitative savings remain
unmeasured without comparable usage records.

## What a credible paper would still need

This list is a research backlog, not an added gate on completing the product.

1. **Prior-work comparison.** Conduct a literature review before claiming novelty. Compare
   observation privileges, action interfaces, teacher access and task definitions fairly.
2. **Prospective held-out evaluation.** Freeze candidates and metrics before new origins open.
   Report wins, costs, faints, PP, illegal actions, recovery and interventions; perfection
   is not expected in a stochastic game. Use origin-aware uncertainty, not turn-count
   pseudo-replication or consumed evaluations recast as unseen.
3. **Controlled attribution.** Compare teacher versus learner continuation; learned combat
   versus a declared baseline; preparation versus no preparation at matched budgets.
   Separate improved infrastructure from improvements caused by learning.
4. **Long-horizon goal evaluation.** Compare learned and fixed goal selection under identical
   opportunity/resource budgets, including failed searches and intervention rates.
5. **Fresh Red completion.** One authenticated start, consequential model-directed progression,
   Champion/Hall of Fame, the 124 native registrations in that save and deferred ledger.
   One completion would establish feasibility, not a general success probability.
6. **Transfer only when measured.** On an unfamiliar compatible hack and later Crystal,
   measure initial performance before adaptation, then teaching cost against a learner
   without Red experience. Report adapter engineering separately from learned transfer.
7. **Reproducibility package and authorship.** Audit licenses and lawful asset requirements;
   provide sanitized protocols, manifests and shareable artifacts where permitted.
   Disclose human direction, AI development assistance and supported/manual actions.

Possible eventual structure: problem and scope → system/authority → training protocols →
controlled results → end-to-end case study → failure analysis → transfer → limitations.
A narrower engineering case study may be appropriate before broad transfer is established;
publication suitability and scientific novelty are presently unassessed.
